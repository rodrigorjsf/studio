# Roles and End-to-End Pipeline of a Professional Video / Post-Production Studio

Scope: agency or boutique post house producing marketing, YouTube and social video for clients. Goal: a role and stage map detailed enough to model as a chain of AI-agent "personas". Source dates: the Frame.io Workflow Guide dates from 2018 (launched Dec 2018, per No Film School) and the Frame.io post-supervisor article from Jan 2020. Both are older, but they are the canonical long-form references. Agency, short-form and freelance sources are mostly from 2025–2026.

---

## 1. Which roles exist, and what does each decide, produce and approve?

### Takeaway
A real studio has about six role families: **client-facing** (account manager/client, producer), **creative-authority** (creative director, art director, director, strategist), **writing** (scriptwriter/copywriter), **execution** (assistant editor, editor, motion designer/animator, VFX, colorist, sound designer/mixer, composer, captioner, thumbnail designer), **coordination** (post supervisor, project manager) and **evaluation** (QC, internal review, client approval). Approval authority runs upward: the production company refers to the agency, the agency to the creative director, and the client has the final word.

### Cited Findings

**Approval hierarchy**
- "the production company refers to the agency, the agency refers to the creative director, and the client has the final word." — [Mini Fridge Media, Agency workflow guide](https://www.minifridgemedia.com/blog/video-production-workflow-agency-guide/)
- Clear decision rights about **who consolidates feedback** prevent bottlenecks and endless revision rounds. — [search summary of agency-brief guides, e.g. Ivory Media](https://ivorymedia.com.au/how-to-brief-video-production-company/); [Media Village](https://www.media-village.co.uk/video/how-to-brief-a-video-production-agency/)

**Creative authority**
- **Creative Director** determines the brand's or project's creative strategy, communicates the vision, and approves project direction so it matches client needs. — [School of Motion, Motion design roles](https://schoolofmotion.com/blog/motion-design-industry-roles-responsibilities) (date not stated)
- **Art Director** "translates the creative strategy, branding and messaging...into a visual road map" and approves designers' work. — [School of Motion](https://schoolofmotion.com/blog/motion-design-industry-roles-responsibilities)
- **Producer** makes the logistical and business decisions: "planning, coordinating and supervising pre-production, production and post-production". The producer is accountable for budget and on-time delivery. — [School of Motion](https://schoolofmotion.com/blog/motion-design-industry-roles-responsibilities)
- **Designer** decides visual composition and receives approval from the AD/CD. **Animator** decides motion and timing and receives feedback from the director/AD. — [School of Motion](https://schoolofmotion.com/blog/motion-design-industry-roles-responsibilities)
- School of Motion does not cover account or client-facing roles (noted as absent from that source).
- Wistia's in-house team runs on **2 video producers, 1 writer, 1 associate director/copywriter, and 1 creative director**. The CD meets with the rest of the company to make sure the videos meet everyone's goals, and team members swap projects to play to their strengths. — [Wistia, Behind the scenes of our in-house video team](https://wistia.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-video-team)

**Post-production coordination**
- The **Post-Production Supervisor** is "responsible for coordinating all post departments, managing the schedule and budget", and is the link between the production office and the post team. Ideally they join before shooting and set up the workflow and cutting room with the editor or assistant editors. — [The Green Shot](https://www.thegreenshot.io/uncategorized/post-production-workflow/); [Frame.io Insider, 2020](https://blog.frame.io/2020/01/27/post-production-supervisor/)
- The post supervisor oversees picture editorial, sound editorial and mixing, music (composition, supervision, editorial, licensing), VFX, graphics and titles, finishing and deliverables. They decide budget allocation, crew assignments and schedule changes. — [Frame.io Insider, 2020](https://blog.frame.io/2020/01/27/post-production-supervisor/)
- The post supervisor keeps version control and makes sure "editors are working with the latest conforms". — [The Green Shot](https://www.thegreenshot.io/uncategorized/post-production-workflow/)
- Frame.io's guide makes the Post Supervisor the owner of the Delivery stage. — [Frame.io Workflow Guide](https://workflow.frame.io/guide/)

**Execution roles**
- **Assistant Editor (AE)** syncs audio, organizes files into bins, isolates all usable takes, and handles the editor's extra requests. The AE also builds **stringouts**, rough sequences of selects shown by scene without music or effects. — [FSU Film Handbook / search summary](https://fsufilmhandbook.com/assistant-editing-workflow/); [Frame.io Workflow Guide](https://workflow.frame.io/guide/)
- The AE organizes dailies, manages metadata, maintains project files, and prepares material for editorial and review. — [Frame.io Workflow Guide](https://workflow.frame.io/guide/)
- **Lead Editor** sets how the project is organized, oversees editorial, receives feedback, and manages relinking from proxies to originals before the conform. — [Frame.io Workflow Guide](https://workflow.frame.io/guide/)
- **Editor** builds and refines the cut with the director. — [The Green Shot](https://www.thegreenshot.io/uncategorized/post-production-workflow/)
- **Video editors** assemble, correct and polish visual and audio media into the final product, following the storyboard or script. In small teams they also manage assets, mix audio, add transitional motion elements and grade color. — [Noble Desktop, Video editor job description](https://www.nobledesktop.com/careers/video-editor/job-description)
- **Motion designer vs editor:** a motion designer creates animated graphics from scratch, while a video editor assembles existing footage into a story. — [Moonb](https://www.moonb.io/blog/motion-designer-and-video-editor); [ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/e/motion-graphics-designer-what-is-the-difference-between-motion-graphics-designer-vs-video-editor)
- **Colorist** works from the conformed project on original camera files and uses reference video to check the conform. — [Frame.io Workflow Guide](https://workflow.frame.io/guide/). The colorist grades under the DP's direction. — [The Green Shot](https://www.thegreenshot.io/uncategorized/post-production-workflow/)
- **Sound Supervisor / Sound department** manages all of sound post: dialogue editing, ADR, foley, effects, music editing and the re-recording mix. Sound receives audio via AAF. — [The Green Shot](https://www.thegreenshot.io/uncategorized/post-production-workflow/); [Frame.io Workflow Guide](https://workflow.frame.io/guide/)
- **VFX Supervisor** oversees the integration of visual effects. **Composer** writes the original score. — [The Green Shot](https://www.thegreenshot.io/uncategorized/post-production-workflow/)
- **DIT** runs checksum-verified backups on set and creates dailies. — [Frame.io Workflow Guide](https://workflow.frame.io/guide/)
- **Finishing artist/colorist** owns the conform: relinking to originals, timeline export (EDL/XML/AAF), and error checking with difference blend modes. — [Frame.io Workflow Guide](https://workflow.frame.io/guide/)
- Other post roles Frame.io lists: sound editors, VFX editors, music editors, colorists, graphics specialists. — [Frame.io Insider, 2020](https://blog.frame.io/2020/01/27/post-production-supervisor/)

**YouTube / creator-team roles**
- **Thumbnail designer** makes the click-driving image. **Lead editor** polishes the footage until it is ready to publish. **Scriptwriter** writes scripts and outlines. **YouTube producer** manages how videos are made and makes sure they are ready to film. **Strategist** pitches series, checks that content fits the brand and the algorithm, and suggests alternate titles and thumbnails after publishing. — [vidIQ, YouTube jobs](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-jobs-content-creators/)
- Mario Joos lists "20 different roles on a YouTube team" (X thread). The full list could not be retrieved because the page returned HTTP 402. — [X/@MarioJoos](https://x.com/MarioJoos/status/1703757187321937994)

**Captioner (accessibility)**
- The FCC's caption quality standards are **accuracy, synchronicity, completeness, placement**. Captions must be verbatim (no paraphrasing), with correct spelling and punctuation, and must convey tone. They must match the spoken words in time and be readable at their speed. — [3Play Media on FCC standards](https://www.3playmedia.com/blog/fccs-new-quality-standards-closed-captioning-video-programming/); [FCC 14-12](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/fcc-14-12a1.pdf)
- DCMP Captioning Key covers accuracy, consistency, clarity, readability and equal access, and requires non-speech sounds to be captioned. A caption frame is typically **2 lines of 32 characters**, and the industry spelling standard is **99% accuracy**. — [3Play Media on DCMP](https://www.3playmedia.com/blog/dcmp-closed-captioning-standards/)

### Inferences
- For an agent chain, the roles collapse into these candidate personas:
  1. **Account/Client liaison** (intake, consolidates feedback, final sign-off proxy)
  2. **Producer / PM** (scope, schedule, revision budget, gatekeeper of rounds)
  3. **Strategist** (platform, audience, hooks, titles/thumbnails; short-form/YouTube-specific)
  4. **Creative Director** (concept, creative strategy, approves direction)
  5. **Art Director** (visual road map / style frames, approves design output)
  6. **Scriptwriter/Copywriter**
  7. **Assistant Editor** (ingest, sync, transcribe, log, selects, stringout)
  8. **Editor** (assembly → fine cut)
  9. **Motion Designer/Animator** (graphics built from scratch)
  10. **Colorist**
  11. **Sound designer/mixer** (plus music selection, since there is often no composer)
  12. **Captioner**
  13. **Thumbnail designer**
  14. **QC**
  15. **Post Supervisor** (orchestrator of post departments and versions; can merge with Producer)
- Two roles are *evaluative by nature*, and in an agent system they should be separate from the maker (a critic is not its own author): **CD/AD (creative review)** and **QC (technical review)**. The **client** is the external evaluator.
- The "who consolidates feedback" finding suggests a dedicated feedback-consolidation step: one persona merges the notes into a single list, then routes it.

- Found no authoritative 2023–2026 source that formally defines the Account Manager role for video agencies. Its role (intake, relationship, upsell, feedback consolidation) is inferred from agency process pages.
- No authoritative job-description source for a dedicated **QC operator** in marketing/social post houses (the role is common in broadcast). In boutiques, QC is usually done by the editor or producer; this is inferred, not sourced.

## 2. What are the canonical pipeline stages, and what are the deliverables and approval gates per stage?

### Takeaway
The canonical chain is: Discovery/Brief → Creative brief → Treatment/Concept → Script (1–2 feedback rounds) → Storyboard/Shot list (sign-off before production spend) → Production (dailies, sign-off) → Ingest/Prep → Logging/Selects/Stringout → Assembly → Rough cut → Fine cut → Client cut → **Picture lock** (the hard gate) → Graphics/VFX, Color, Sound in parallel → Captions → Conform/Online → QC → Delivery (masters + versions) → Archive. Agencies typically take 6–9 weeks end to end, of which 4–6 weeks is post.

### Cited Findings

**Pre-production**
- A discovery conversation covers audience, goals, competitive context and measurement criteria. The team then builds a **creative brief** and project outline capturing message, tone and visual direction. — [search summary of agency-process pages, e.g. Indirap](https://www.indirap.com/blog/what-to-expect-from-a-full-service-video-production-process); [TriVision](https://trivisionstudios.com/corporate-video-production-process-explained-step-by-step/)
- Teams typically allow **1–2 feedback rounds on the script** and up to **3 rounds on the edit**. — [agency-brief guide search summary](https://www.epitomecollective.com/blog/what-is-video-production-brief)
- Agency phases and durations:

| Phase | Duration | Primary owner |
|---|---|---|
| Script interpretation & pre-production prep | 1–2 weeks | production company |
| Pre-production planning | 1–2 weeks | production company + agency |
| Production | 1–2 days | production company, agency oversight |
| Post-production & refinement | 4–6 weeks | production co + agency + client |
| Delivery | 1–3 days | production company |

  Total **6–9 weeks**. — [Mini Fridge Media](https://www.minifridgemedia.com/blog/video-production-workflow-agency-guide/)
- Phase 1 outputs: **shot list, storyboard, action plan**, all needing agency sign-off. Gates: **post-storyboard (before production costs)**, the pre-production review meeting, and **daily shoot sign-off**. — [Mini Fridge Media](https://www.minifridgemedia.com/blog/video-production-workflow-agency-guide/)

**Capture and prep (ingest)**
- Frame.io's guide has 9 chapters: **Capture, Prep, Edit, Conform, VFX, Color, Sound, Delivery, Case Studies**, over 100,000 words. — [Jonny Elwyn on Frame.io guide](https://jonnyelwyn.co.uk/film-and-video-editing/inside-frame-ios-ultimate-post-production-workflow-guide/); [Frame.io guide](https://workflow.frame.io/guide/)
- Prep covers checksum backups, dailies creation and organization, dual-system sound sync, metadata, transcoding to edit-friendly codecs (proxies), LUT application, and delivery to editors and reviewers. — [Frame.io Workflow Guide](https://workflow.frame.io/guide/)
- "Dailies (also referred to as 'rushes') is the collective term for all of the footage shot on a given day". — [Frame.io Workflow Guide](https://workflow.frame.io/guide/)

**Editorial stages** (from [Frame.io, Editing stages](https://workflow.frame.io/guide/editing-stages))
- **Assembly**: built by the editor, often during production. It stays close to the script, includes all shot material, and often runs **about twice the final length**. It has no VFX, color, sound design or temp music. Directors benefit from a fast turnaround so they can spot reshoots before wrap.
- **Rough cut**: the editor and director work closely, and producers may give feedback. "Most of the blood, sweat, and tears of the editing process are shed" getting from assembly to rough cut. Placeholder VFX and rough sound appear. It is shown to producers and clients, though "the client for a marketing video...may not have the experience to judge it properly."
- **Intermediate cuts**: first cut (test screening), fine cut (producer review), director's cut, producer's cut.
- **Picture lock**: "No cuts can be altered". Locking is what lets color, sound and finishing start.
- The fine cut refines transitions, trims and pacing. Picture lock lets sound, color and VFX work without disruption. — [MASV workflow guide](https://massive.io/workflow/post-production-workflow/); [Indie Shorts Mag, 2026](https://www.indieshortsmag.com/tutorials/post-production/2026/01/ultimate-guide-to-short-film-post-production/)
- Agency version sequence: **assembly → rough cut → fine cut → client cut → picture lock**. — [Mini Fridge Media](https://www.minifridgemedia.com/blog/video-production-workflow-agency-guide/)

**Post-lock (parallel departments)**
- VFX, sound and color work from the locked picture, and changes after lock are costly. "Multiple departments work simultaneously and interdependently"; delays cascade downstream. — [The Green Shot](https://www.thegreenshot.io/uncategorized/post-production-workflow/)
- Color grading has two parts: **correction** (consistency and accuracy across shots) and **creative grade** (look and mood). — [Indie Shorts Mag, 2026](https://www.indieshortsmag.com/tutorials/post-production/2026/01/ultimate-guide-to-short-film-post-production/)
- Sound covers dialogue cleanup, SFX, level balancing and music. — [same](https://www.indieshortsmag.com/tutorials/post-production/2026/01/ultimate-guide-to-short-film-post-production/)
- **Conform** bridges editorial and finishing: reference video export, relinking to camera originals, timeline simplification, EDL/XML/AAF export, and error checks with difference blend mode. — [Frame.io Workflow Guide](https://workflow.frame.io/guide/)

**Approval gates (consolidated list)**
- Assembly review, rough cut approval, fine cut sign-off, picture lock confirmation, VFX spotting, sound mix approval, color grade sign-off, final delivery sign-off. — [The Green Shot](https://www.thegreenshot.io/uncategorized/post-production-workflow/)
- Scheduling checkpoints: **Rough Cut, Picture Lock, Final Mix, QC, Master Delivery**. QC gates cover technical checks after key exports, creative reviews between rough/fine/locked cuts, and pre-delivery audio and compliance checks. — [Farmerswife, Post-production scheduling](https://blog.farmerswife.com/post-production-scheduling-steps)

**QC and technical specs**
- Loudness targets: **EBU R128 −23 LUFS ±1 LU, true peak −1 dBTP** (EU broadcast); **ATSC A/85 −24 LKFS ±2 LU** (US broadcast, practical TP ceiling −2 dBTP); about **−14 LUFS** for streaming/YouTube. — [NUGEN Audio](https://nugenaudio.com/broadcast-loudness-made-simple/); [Production Expert, 2026](https://www.production-expert.com/production-expert-1/what-is-loudness-lufs-lkfs-and-delivery-specs-explained-2026)
- Pre-export checklist: LUFS on target, true peak under the platform limit, LRA suited to the content, metadata embedded, compliance passed. — [same sources, search summary](https://www.forte-ai.com/blog/post-audio-deliverables-checklist-broadcast-streaming-theatrical)
- Captions: see §1 (FCC 4 standards; 2×32 chars; 99% spelling accuracy).

**Delivery**
- Typical agency delivery bundle: **master (4K ProRes), web version (1080p H.264), social cuts, subtitles (.SRT/.VTT), thumbnails, raw footage**. — [Mini Fridge Media](https://www.minifridgemedia.com/blog/video-production-workflow-agency-guide/)
- Long-form deliverables also include multiple formats, languages, subtitles and metadata. — [The Green Shot](https://www.thegreenshot.io/uncategorized/post-production-workflow/)

**Archive**
- The 3-2-1 rule (3 copies, 2 different media, 1 off-site) "remains the foundation of professional post-production backup". Archive both the sources (raw, audio, graphics, music) and the outputs (ProRes/DNx/H.264/HEVC masters). Back up project files daily or after each major change, and outputs after each delivery milestone. — [MASV, 3-2-1 for media](https://massive.io/content-security/3-2-1-backup-rule/); [LinkedIn advice (low authority)](https://www.linkedin.com/advice/1/what-best-ways-archive-backup-post-production-1z5me)
- Netflix's production data guidelines require original camera files and original production audio on at least 2 distinct media (NVMe, RAID, LTO 6–9 in LTFS, cloud). — [MASV](https://massive.io/content-security/3-2-1-backup-rule/) (secondary citation of Netflix)

**Tooling and change control**
- A central hub (e.g. Google Drive) holds documents, feedback and versions. Verbal decisions are confirmed by email, scope additions need written approval, and file naming and versioning are enforced. — [Mini Fridge Media](https://www.minifridgemedia.com/blog/video-production-workflow-agency-guide/)
- Cloud review platforms give centralized feedback and approval tracking for distributed teams. — [The Green Shot](https://www.thegreenshot.io/uncategorized/post-production-workflow/)

### Inferences
- **Picture lock** is the key architectural seam for an agent chain. Everything before it is a creative loop with the client; everything after it is parallel, specialized, lower-ambiguity work (color, sound, graphics, captions) that can fan out.
- Every gate follows the same pattern (artifact → reviewer → approve/request changes, with a counted round). That lends itself to a generic "gate" primitive in the plugin.
- The local repo's existing rules (`locked-final-cut`, word-level timing from `palavras.json`, mandatory approval of `plano.md` and review stills) already map onto stages 12, 13 and 18 of this table. For a talking-head-over-graphics workflow, the recorded master acts as an already-locked picture.

## 3. How do small studios and solo editors compress these roles, and which are essential vs optional for short-form social?

### Takeaway
Small teams merge roles into a few hats: Producer+Account, CD+Writer, Editor+AE+Motion+Color+Sound+Captions. Wistia runs a whole in-house studio on 5 people. For short-form social, the essential chain is **Strategy/hook → Script → Edit (fast pacing) → Captions → Platform formatting → Performance analytics/A/B**. Color grading, dedicated sound mixing, conform and formal QC become light or optional. Hook testing, captions and repurposing become essential.

### Cited Findings
- In small or generalist settings, a video editor's job includes managing assets, mixing audio, creating transitional motion design and color grading. In a larger team the same title narrows to one function (e.g. SFX, color, cleanup). — [Noble Desktop](https://www.nobledesktop.com/careers/video-editor/job-description)
- Wistia's 5-person in-house team (2 producers, 1 writer, 1 associate director/copywriter, 1 CD) swaps projects to play to strengths. In-house teams must "adjust at the drop of a dime" rather than follow a predictable schedule. — [Wistia](https://wistia.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-video-team)
- Short-form agencies cover **content strategy, scripting and hooks, filming and editing, platform-specific optimization, performance tracking, and influencer or creator partnerships**. — [inBeat Agency, Apr/Jul 2026](https://inbeat.agency/blog/top-short-form-video-agencies)
- Short-form post means "professional editing with fast pacing and captions", motion graphics, platform-specific formatting, **A/B testing on hooks, thumbnails and creative formats**, and ROI-driven refinement. High-performing agencies produce **20–40 videos a month**. — [inBeat, 2026](https://inbeat.agency/blog/top-short-form-video-agencies)
- Repurposing: one long-form video can yield **30+ social posts**. The five-stage workflow is strategic planning, clip identification, platform-native editing, distribution, performance analysis. A 7-day cadence runs: Day 1 generate 15–30 clip candidates; Day 2 pick the top 10–20 and write hook headlines; Day 3 edit the first 5 vertical clips; Day 5 add captions and export without watermarks. — [quso.ai, 2026](https://quso.ai/blog/how-to-repurpose-video-content) (vendor blog, lower authority)
- A YouTube strategist suggests alternate titles and thumbnails after publishing, which makes post-publish iteration a real stage. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-jobs-content-creators/)
- Frame.io warns that marketing-video clients may not be able to judge a rough cut properly. — [Frame.io](https://workflow.frame.io/guide/editing-stages)

### Essential vs optional (inferred from the findings above)

| Role / stage | Long-form corporate/brand | YouTube long-form | Short-form social |
|---|---|---|---|
| Account / client liaison | essential | optional (creator = client) | essential for agencies |
| Strategist (platform, hooks, titles) | optional | essential | **essential** |
| Creative Director review | essential | essential (often creator) | essential (light) |
| Scriptwriter | essential | essential | essential (hook-first, short) |
| Storyboard / shot list | essential | optional | optional |
| AE (ingest/log/transcribe/selects) | essential | essential | essential (transcript-driven clip finding) |
| Editor | essential | essential | essential |
| Motion designer | usually | usually | usually (text/kinetic type) |
| Colorist | essential | light | light/optional |
| Sound mixer | essential (loudness spec) | light (−14 LUFS) | light |
| Captioner | essential (accessibility/legal) | essential | **essential (burned-in)** |
| Thumbnail designer | optional | **essential** | optional (cover frame) |
| Formal QC | essential | light | light (checklist) |
| Post supervisor | medium/large only | rarely | rarely (producer covers it) |
| Performance analytics / A/B | rare | essential | **essential** |

### Inferences
- An agent plugin for a solo creator should default to a **compressed chain**: Director (orchestrator = producer + CD + client liaison) → Strategist → Writer → AE → Editor → Motion → Finishing (color + sound + captions) → QC critic → Delivery. Specialist personas should activate only when the format needs them (e.g. no colorist for screen recordings).
- Keeping the **critic/QC persona separate** from the maker is the one role *not* to compress. Solo editors lose this independent check, and it is what agent systems can cheaply add back.

### Gaps
- Found no quantitative survey (2023–2026) of how many roles small studios actually staff. The compression model rests on job descriptions and case examples, not survey data.

## 4. Typical revision rounds and gatekeeping practices

### Takeaway
The norm is **1–2 rounds on the script and 2–3 rounds on the edit** (rough → fine → client/final). A "round" means one set of consolidated feedback followed by one new version. Anything beyond that, or any change of concept, footage or versions, is a paid scope change that needs written approval. The main gates are storyboard sign-off (before production spend) and picture lock (before finishing).

### Cited Findings
- Professional production companies build in **2–3 revision rounds**: rough cut, then fine cut, then final. Round 1 covers major and structural notes; round 2 covers fine-tuning. — [agency process search summary](https://www.media-village.co.uk/video/how-to-brief-a-video-production-agency/); [Ivory Media](https://ivorymedia.com.au/how-to-brief-video-production-company/)
- **Three defined revision rounds** apply before extra charges: (1) rough cut, reviewed internally first; (2) fine cut with agency feedback; (3) client cut with client notes. Additional revisions need a documented scope change and cost assessment. — [Mini Fridge Media](https://www.minifridgemedia.com/blog/video-production-workflow-agency-guide/)
- Freelance contracts: "a round of revisions equals consolidated feedback and changes implemented with a new version delivered". **2–3 rounds** are usually included, with extra rounds billed per round or hourly. A single indecisive client can turn "a 6-hour project into a 30-hour nightmare". — [Bonsai](https://www.hellobonsai.com/contract-template/video-editing); [Pixflow 2026](https://pixflow.net/blog/freelance-video-editing-rates/); [OneSuite 2026](https://onesuite.io/blog/video-editing-contract/)
- **Revision vs scope change**: tweaking the grade, changing music or adjusting transitions counts as a revision. Adding footage, making extra versions or changing the core concept is a scope change and carries a fee. — [same freelance-contract sources](https://pixflow.net/blog/freelance-video-editing-rates/)
- Script: 1–2 feedback rounds. — [Epitome Collective](https://www.epitomecollective.com/blog/what-is-video-production-brief)
- Gate before production spend: storyboard sign-off. Gate before finishing: picture lock ("no cuts can be altered"). — [Mini Fridge Media](https://www.minifridgemedia.com/blog/video-production-workflow-agency-guide/); [Frame.io](https://workflow.frame.io/guide/editing-stages)
- Governance practices: one person consolidates feedback; verbal decisions are confirmed in writing; version naming is enforced. — [Mini Fridge Media](https://www.minifridgemedia.com/blog/video-production-workflow-agency-guide/); [Ivory Media](https://ivorymedia.com.au/how-to-brief-video-production-company/)
- Internal review comes before the client sees a cut ("rough cut (internal production review first)"). — [Mini Fridge Media](https://www.minifridgemedia.com/blog/video-production-workflow-agency-guide/)

### Inferences
- Model each gate as: **maker produces a versioned artifact → internal critic (CD/QC) reviews → consolidated notes → client approves or requests changes → round counter increments**. The producer persona enforces the round budget and classifies each note as a revision or a scope change.
- Internal review before the client is standard. An agent chain should run the internal critic pass *before* every user-facing approval point, so the user is spared obvious fixes.
- Post-lock changes are expensive in real studios. In an agent/Remotion workflow they are cheap to re-render but still invalidate downstream work (captions, timing, QC), so the lock concept should survive as a state flag.
