# Client Briefing, Technical Spec Alignment, and Brand Identity Handling in Professional Video Studios

Scope: how studios, agencies and freelance editors align with a client *before* editing starts. The goal is a catalogue of questions an AI "grilling" interview can run. Each topic is tagged **[brand/stable]** (set once per client or brand, reused across jobs) or **[per-video]** (changes with each job).

Source-date caveats: StudioBinder's template is from 2017, before the preferred 2023–2026 window. It is kept only because it is a widely copied baseline. The Haikai delivery cheat sheet shows "August 28" with no year. The Frame.io MOGRT article is from May 2023. All other fetched sources are from 2025–2026.

---

## Q1. What do real video briefing / onboarding questionnaires and creative brief templates ask?

### Takeaway
Published brief templates agree on a core of about 10 fields: background/overview, objective, audience, key message, tone/style (with references), distribution channels, deliverables and specs, timeline, budget, and success metrics. Newer templates (2025–2026) add stakeholders and approval workflow, "clippable moments" for repurposing, copy/CTA, and a revision-rounds definition. Legal topics (usage rights, music licensing, talent releases) sit in the production *contract* rather than the creative brief.

### Cited Findings
- **StudioBinder (2017) creative brief, 11 sections:**
  1. Overview (big picture plus previous similar campaigns)
  2. Objectives ("concrete and attainable", measurable)
  3. Target audience (demographics and buyer behavior)
  4. Competition (competitors' approaches)
  5. Message (distinct from product benefits)
  6. Tone (humorous, musical, etc., matching brand personality)
  7. Visuals (mood boards, storyboard slides)
  8. Scheduling
  9. Budget
  10. Dates/deadlines per pre-production, production and post-production phase
  11. "Stray observations" (special requirements)

  — [StudioBinder](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/best-creative-brief-template-video-agencies-free-download/)
- **Ziflow video brief template (Aug 2025):**
  - Project info: name, owner, stakeholders
  - Background
  - Objective
  - Storyboard or script outline
  - Target audience: demographics, psychographics, specific characteristics
  - Distribution channels, including social network specs
  - Deliverables, each with a due date and owner
  - Technical requirements: resolution, orientation (horizontal/vertical), file format, maximum file size, special effects, graphic design/animation needs, interactive elements
  - Copy to include: script, tagline, subtitles, social captions, CTA
  - Deadline and priority level

  It has no dedicated approvals or legal section. — [Ziflow](https://www.ziflow.com/blog/video-brief-template)
- **Goldcast video creative brief (Nov 2025):**
  - Project and admin: name, stakeholders (owner, production team, creative lead, marketing leader), key deadlines, **approval workflow**
  - Goals: primary (conversion), secondary (engagement), systematic (amplification)
  - Audience: persona/role, pain points, motivations, **stage in the customer journey**
  - Messaging: "single most important message" plus "3–5 additional 'clippable' moments"
  - Creative requirements: tone and style; visual and brand guidelines (colors, graphics, lighting); talent and speakers; format notes
  - Distribution: primary deliverable, repurposed deliverables, channels and posting schedule
  - KPIs (awareness, engagement, conversion) and CRM/MAP data workflows

  — [Goldcast](https://www.goldcast.io/blog-post/video-creative-brief-template)
- **Celtx (Mar 2026, updated May 2026), 7 essentials:** project overview, target audience, key message, tone and style (with references), deliverables (primary video, social edits, vertical versions), timeline and milestones, success metrics (views, engagement, conversions). Celtx says the brief "should be completed before scripting begins." — [Celtx](https://blog.celtx.com/how-to-write-a-creative-brief-video/)
- **Creator/YouTube context (Creator's Assistant, updated Sep 2026).** What a creator should hand an editor:
  - a style guide ("fonts, colors, graphics, captions, and examples")
  - "three to five finished videos with timestamped notes", plus audience expectations and the channel promise
  - the deliverable definition ("one finished 16:9 video"), captions, audio cleanup expectations, B-roll usage, revision rounds, project files, export formats
  - whether the editor follows detailed instructions or uses creative judgment
  - turnaround for rough cut, graphics, review and final
  - "one place for feedback" with "timestamped comments"
  - revision limits: "the number of included rounds, what counts as a revision, what counts as a new request"
  - handoff of "project files and final export"

  — [The Creator's Assistant](https://www.thecreatorsassistant.com/how-to-hire-youtube-video-editor)
- **Contract and scoping (Minifridge Media, May 2026):**
  - "Three rounds of revisions before additional changes are charged"
  - Revisions (edits within the approved direction) are distinct from scope changes (structural, needing more budget)
  - "Consolidate feedback before sending it"
  - Usage rights: limited vs unlimited, territory, exclusivity; actor buyouts typically 1–2 years per platform
  - Raw footage handoff
  - Music/stock licensing must match the intended platforms
  - Standard package: 4K 16:9 master, shorter 16:9 and 9:16 cuts, social versions with burned-in captions
  - Payment: 30–50% deposit, then milestone, then final payment on delivery

  — [Minifridge Media](https://www.minifridgemedia.com/blog/video-production-contracts/)
- **Contract topics across agreement templates (search summary only; claims not individually verified per URL):**
  - Vague deliverables such as "final video" without formats, resolutions or versions are a red flag.
  - Cap the number of revision rounds.
  - Clarify who owns final videos, raw footage, project files, licensed music, VO, stock and "AI-assisted or third-party creative elements".
  - Music license scope: medium, territory, term, language, exclusivity.
  - Anyone recognizable needs a signed talent release before commercial use.

  — search results across [ailawyer.pro](https://ailawyer.pro/templates/video-production-agreement), [fynk](https://fynk.com/en/templates/video-production-agreement/), [krock.io](https://krock.io/blog/navigating-legal-and-ethical-issues-in-video-production/), [terms.law](https://terms.law/2023/11/09/the-essential-guide-to-contracts-for-video-production/)
- **Videographer onboarding questionnaire sections (product listing, low authority):** client info, project info, services, expectations and communication, logistics. — [Etsy listing](https://www.etsy.com/listing/1466784516/videography-services-client-onboarding)

### Inferences
Catalogue of briefing topics for the grilling agent, grouped by theme:

**A. Business and context**
- [brand/stable] What the business or channel is. The channel promise. Competitors and how they do video (StudioBinder, Creator's Assistant).
- [per-video] Background: why this video now, and previous similar pieces (StudioBinder, Ziflow).

**B. Objective and KPIs**
- [per-video] Primary and secondary goal (sell, sign up, awareness, educate). The single metric of success (Goldcast, Celtx).
- [per-video] Stage of the customer journey (Goldcast).

**C. Audience**
- [brand/stable] Core persona: demographics, psychographics, pain points, motivations (Ziflow, Goldcast).
- [per-video] Whether this video targets a sub-segment.

**D. Message and copy**
- [per-video] The one message the viewer must remember. The 3–5 "clippable" moments (Goldcast).
- [per-video] Script, tagline, CTA wording, on-screen copy, and social post captions (Ziflow).
- [brand/stable] Standardized CTAs, approved terms, and phrases to avoid (Zelios, see Q3).

**E. Tone and references**
- [brand/stable] Brand personality and default tone.
- [per-video] Tone for this piece. 3–5 reference videos with timestamped notes on what to copy (Creator's Assistant, Celtx).

**F. Distribution**
- [per-video] Which platforms, primary vs repurposed cuts, and posting schedule (Goldcast, Ziflow). This feeds Q2 specs.

**G. Deliverables, timeline and budget**
- [per-video] The list of files. Due date per milestone (rough cut, review, final). Priority. Budget (Ziflow, StudioBinder, Creator's Assistant).

**H. Process and approvals**
- [brand/stable] Who approves, how many revision rounds, what counts as a revision vs a new request, a single consolidated feedback channel with timestamped notes (Goldcast, Creator's Assistant, Minifridge).
- [per-video] Any extra stakeholder for this job.

**I. Legal and rights**
- [per-video] Music, stock, talent releases, usage territory and term, and ownership of project files and AI-generated elements (Minifridge, contract-template search summary).

**J. Creative latitude**
- [brand/stable] Whether the editor follows the instructions literally or has freedom to propose (Creator's Assistant).

Other patterns across the sources:
- Legal and rights topics are consistently *absent* from creative brief templates (Ziflow and Celtx explicitly omit them) and appear in contracts. An AI interview that merges brief and contract covers ground that studios usually split across two documents.
- In creator workflows the brief is thin and the stable style guide carries most of the load. Corporate workflows are the reverse: a heavy per-project brief, with brand guidelines referenced by link.

### Gaps
- Brief/questionnaire templates from HubSpot, Wistia, Vidyard, Frame.io (brief-specific), Motion Array and Envato were not retrieved. Their exact question lists are unverified.
- Kill fees, cancellation terms and AI-disclosure clauses were not found in a fetched source.

## Q2. Technical alignment topics

### Takeaway
Technical alignment comes down to a "delivery spec sheet": per-platform aspect ratio, resolution, fps, codec, color space, loudness, captions form (burned-in vs sidecar), and file naming. Creator-side editing style adds pacing targets, transitions, text cards, still-image motion, music level/ducking, and a do-not list. Safe zones for 9:16 platforms are concrete, platform-specific pixel margins. Sources disagree on the exact Reels numbers.

### Cited Findings
- **Corporate delivery spec checklist (Haikai, year not stated):**
  - Master: ProRes 422 HQ, full resolution
  - Deliverables: H.264 in MP4
  - Resolution: 3840×2160 or 1920×1080
  - Frame rate: 25 fps (EU), 29.97 (US) or 23.976 (cinematic)
  - Color: "graded and rendered in Rec 709"
  - Shapes: 16:9 (1920×1080), 9:16 (1080×1920), 4:5 (1080×1350), 1:1 (1080×1080)
  - Audio: stereo at −14 LUFS integrated for online, EBU R128 −23 LUFS for broadcast; split stems / M&E version recommended
  - Captions: SRT sidecar with all deliverables, burned-in captions for social versions
  - File naming: "project, version and shape in every filename, with one final version clearly marked"

  The page does not cover bitrate, alpha, textless versions or thumbnails. — [Haikai Media](https://www.haikai.media/post/corporate-video-delivery-specs-a-cheat-sheet-for-clients-who-commission-video)
- **Loudness by destination (search summary, not individually verified):**
  - US broadcast (ATSC A/85): −24 LKFS ±1
  - EBU R128: −23 LUFS ±0.5
  - Netflix: −27 LKFS dialog-gated, −2 dBTP
  - YouTube normalizes to about −14 LUFS
  - True peak typically ≤ −2 dBFS

  — search results across [The Post Flow](https://thepostflow.com/filmmaker-resources/delivery-specs-cheat-sheet/), [AICP spec PDF](https://aicp.com/assets/editor/AICP_File_Deliverable_Specifications.pdf), [PBSd 2024 specs](https://pbsdistribution.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/PBSd-GLOBAL-TECHNICAL-SPECIFICATIONS-September-2024.pdf)
- **Closed captions** can ship as an embedded QuickTime CC track or as a sidecar SRT. A sidecar lets viewers switch captions off and lets search engines read them (search summary). — same sources as above
- **Brand video guidelines favor sidecar over burn-in.** "Where transcripts/SRT files can be uploaded directly on a platform, opt for this option rather than burning captions into the video." For burn-in, specify font, size and 1–3 lines. For subtitles, no more than 3 lines on screen, and speaker labels / chapter titles on one line (search summary). — [University of St Andrews video guidelines](https://www.st-andrews.ac.uk/brand/design-guidelines/video/), [UAL](https://www.arts.ac.uk/brand/using-our-brand/video)
- **9:16 safe zones (Postplanify, updated Apr 7 2026), all at 1080×1920:**

  | Platform | Safe area | Top | Bottom | Left | Right | Note |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | TikTok | 900×1492 | 108 px | 320 px | 60 px | 120 px | Ads: 370 px bottom |
  | Instagram Reels | 996×1400 | 210 px | 310 px | 0 | 84 px | |
  | Instagram Stories | 1080×1620 | 100 px | 200 px | 0 | 0 | |
  | YouTube Shorts | 984×1500 | 120 px | 300 px | — | 96 px | |

  — [Postplanify](https://postplanify.com/blog/social-media-safe-zones-2026-complete-guide)
- **Conflicting Reels numbers.** Other 2026 guides (search summary) say to keep text and logos away from the top 220 px and bottom 430 px, or "top 14%, bottom 35%, sides 6%". They say the expandable caption, audio label and buttons can claim about 670 px at the bottom. The disagreement probably reflects different UI states (collapsed vs expanded caption, organic vs ad). — [House of Marketers](https://houseofmarketers.com/guide-to-safe-zones-tiktok-facebook-instagram-stories-reels/), [Zeely](https://zeely.ai/blog/master-instagram-safe-zones/), [Kreatli](https://kreatli.com/guides/instagram-reels-safe-zone)
- **Series style guide for editors (Umbrella Creators, Sep 2026):**
  - Format and runtime, aspect ratio, upload cadence
  - **Pacing targets:** "picture changes per minute", median shot length, maximum shot duration, *measured from existing uploads*
  - Default transition (hard cut / cross-dissolve / dip to black) and the exception rules
  - Text cards: font, colors, display duration, and when information is text vs narration only
  - Recurring graphics, their placement, and "what must NOT change between episodes"
  - Still-photo motion (Ken Burns speed and zoom direction)
  - Color and B&W rules, with a graded reference episode
  - Music level under dialogue, target LUFS, ducking, SFX policy
  - Credits and attribution (stock, archive, music, guests)
  - Fixed opening, a **do-not list**, and reference timestamps

  — [Umbrella Creators](https://umbrellacreators.com/resources/youtube-series-style-guide-template)
- **Zelios brand video style guide (May 2026):**
  - Intro/outro animations "Two to three seconds maximum"
  - Pick one transition style; clean cuts suit most brands
  - Lower-third and title-card templates
  - Easing curves
  - Sonic logo and when it plays
  - Music direction written as a spec (example: "Electronic, 120–140 BPM, instrumental only")
  - VO pacing and tone
  - Aspect-ratio variants 16:9 / 9:16 / 1:1

  — [Zelios](https://zelios.agency/brand-video-style-guide/)
- **Creator pacing and zoom heuristics (search summary, not individually verified):**
  - Younger audiences (13–24) need a visual change every 15–25 s; 25+ tolerate 20–40 s holds.
  - Over-editing (endless zooms, stacked jump cuts) tires viewers.
  - A zoom should answer "Where is it?", "What changed?" or "What should I do next?" and land together with the narration or caption.
  - Pattern interrupts (B-roll, on-screen text, zoom cuts, SFX, callouts) every 60–90 s.
  - Niche conventions: gaming uses beat-matched cuts and memes; education uses callouts and clear on-screen text; fitness uses rep counters.

  — search results across [air.io](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/advanced-retention-editing-cutting-patterns-that-keep-viewers-past-minute-8), [CapCut zoom guide](https://www.capcut.com/create/zoom-effects-for-tutorial-videos-guide-attention), [Inside the Edit](https://www.insidetheedit.com/blog/pacing-in-video-editing), [Miracamp](https://www.miracamp.com/learn/video-editing/how-to-become-a-video-editor-for-youtubers)
- **Ziflow technical requirements:** resolution, orientation, file format, max file size, special effects, graphics/animation needs, interactive elements. — [Ziflow](https://www.ziflow.com/blog/video-brief-template)

### Inferences
Technical grilling catalogue. Most of these can be answered once and stored as the brand's defaults:

**Format**
- [per-video] Aspect ratio(s) and whether each is a separate composition or a reframed derivative.
- [brand/stable] Resolution and fps default: match the master unless told otherwise.

**Delivery**
- [per-video] Codec/container per deliverable (MP4 H.264 vs ProRes vs MOV with alpha for overlays), file naming, max file size.
- [brand/stable] Default loudness target (−14 LUFS online) and whether stems are wanted.

**Safe zones**
- [per-video] Target platform, which drives the safe-zone margins above. Keep a per-platform table.
- [brand/stable] A "face-safe" rule for talking-head content.

**Pacing and rhythm**
- [brand/stable] Target visual changes per minute, max hold length, pattern-interrupt cadence, and intensity (subtle / balanced / dominant).
- The Umbrella Creators practice of *measuring* pacing from existing uploads is automatable: an agent can compute cuts per minute from the reference videos.

**Camera behavior**
- [brand/stable] Whether punch-ins/zooms are allowed, how strong, how often, and when (tied to "where / what changed / what next").
- [brand/stable] Still-image motion rules (Ken Burns direction and speed).

**Transitions**
- [brand/stable] Default transition and its exceptions. Intro/outro length (≤ 2–3 s).

**Text and graphics**
- [brand/stable] Text-card font, color, duration, and when to use text vs narration.
- [brand/stable] Lower-third template. Recurring graphics and what must never change.

**Captions**
- [brand/stable] Captions burned-in vs sidecar (SRT); style (font, size, line count, highlight color, position inside the safe zone).
- [per-video] Captions language(s).

**B-roll, stock and screenshots**
- [per-video] Allowed sources (client-provided, stock, AI-generated), licensing, and which claims in the speech need a visual proof such as a screenshot.

**Color**
- [brand/stable] LUT or graded reference and color space (Rec 709).
- [per-video] B&W or special looks.

**Music and SFX**
- [brand/stable] Music genre/BPM spec, pre-approved library, level under dialogue, ducking, SFX policy, sonic logo.
- [per-video] Track choice and licensing.

**Audio cleanup**
- [per-video] Noise reduction, filler removal, and level matching expectations (Creator's Assistant lists "audio cleanup expectations" as a brief item).

**Hooks, CTAs, end screens and thumbnails**
- [per-video] Opening hook and CTA wording (Ziflow "copy to be included").
- [brand/stable] Fixed opening/outro (Umbrella).

**Mapping to this repo's constraints** (inference, not a finding): `studio/CLAUDE.md` treats the recorded master as locked (no cuts, reordering or speed changes), keeps faces free, and requires word-timed triggers. So pro-studio topics must be translated before they are asked:
- "cut rhythm / jump cuts / pacing" becomes overlay density and visual-change rate *on top of* the master.
- "camera movement / punch-ins" becomes scale/transform applied to the master layer.
- "face-safe framing" maps to rule 4 ("Rosto livre").
- "hooks" can only be overlays, never re-ordering.

The grilling agent should either not ask about cutting at all or state that it is out of scope.

### Gaps
- No fetched source for thumbnail specs, YouTube end-screen dimensions and timing, or hook conventions.
- No authoritative source on punch-in/zoom conventions (percent scale, frequency). The heuristics above come from search summaries of blog content.
- Dynamic "creator-style" caption styling (word-by-word highlight, emoji, position) has no fetched source.
- How studios handle screenshots/screen recordings (integrity, highlight, zoom) was not sourced.
- 16:9 title-safe / action-safe margins were not retrieved (Postplanify has no 16:9 guidance).
- Alpha-channel deliverables (ProRes 4444 overlays) and textless versions were not covered by any fetched source.

## Q3. Brand identity / style guide for video; brand kits and how they are stored and reused

### Takeaway
A video style guide extends the static brand book with motion, audio and pacing rules. Practitioners stress that it should "lead with assets, not prose": LUTs, editable lower-thirds, approved music. Tools store the reusable part as a **brand kit** (fonts, colors, logos, media, templates, sometimes a glossary). Kits are usually one per workspace, managed by admins, and applied by copying into each project. Adobe's MOGRTs plus Creative Cloud Libraries are the pro-NLE equivalent, with "only the newest version" distribution.

### Cited Findings
- **Zelios (May 2026), components of a brand video style guide:**
  - Logo placement, minimum size and safe zones, with actual files (transparent PNG, vector, animated)
  - Hex palette
  - A **LUT** for consistent grading
  - Typography with minimum sizes for mobile
  - Intro/outro ≤ 2–3 s; transition style; lower-third and title-card templates; easing curves
  - Sonic logo; music direction; VO guidelines
  - Brand voice, standardized CTAs, approved terminology and phrases to avoid
  - Aspect-ratio variants; downloadable templates and asset library; do's and don'ts; pre-approved music playlists

  Recommended length: 10–15 pages or a single digital workspace that prioritizes downloadable assets. — [Zelios](https://zelios.agency/brand-video-style-guide/)
- **"Assets over prose"** (search summary): "Effective guides lead with assets, not prose — downloadable LUT files, editable lower-third templates, and pre-approved music playlists do more work than any written description." — attributed in search results to [Zelios](https://zelios.agency/brand-video-style-guide/) / [IdeaRocket](https://idearocketanimation.com/15178-video_branding_guidelines/)
- **Institutional video guidelines** exist as public examples: [St Andrews](https://www.st-andrews.ac.uk/brand/design-guidelines/video/), [WashU](https://marcomm.washu.edu/video-brand-guidelines/), [SUNY Geneseo](https://www.geneseo.edu/comm_mark/video-style-guide/), [UAL](https://www.arts.ac.uk/brand/using-our-brand/video), [Adventist Health PDF](https://brand.adventisthealth.org/files/AH-Video-Brand-Standards.pdf). They were not fetched individually; the search summary says they cover lower thirds, captions and cohesive assets.
- **Descript Brand Studio:**
  - Stores fonts (.ttf/.otf/.woff or Google Fonts), colors (exposed in the color picker), logos, **layout packs** (templates remixed with brand fonts and colors, with a default set), up to 500 shared media items (images, video, audio, B-roll), and a **glossary** (transcription terms plus "Do Not Translate" lists).
  - Business/Enterprise plans only; one brand kit per Drive.
  - Owner/Admin manages it; Editors use it; Viewers only view.

  — [Descript Help: Brand Studio](https://help.descript.com/hc/en-us/articles/38106088202765-Brand-Studio)
- **Clipchamp brand kit:**
  - Stores colors and fonts; work accounts also get logos (up to 300), images/videos/graphics/music/SFX (300 each), unlimited fonts (≤ 50 MB each) and colors.
  - Assets are applied by drag-and-drop or picked from font/color dropdowns.
  - "Assets added to a project are copied into that project and remain there even if the original brand kit is later disconnected."

  — [Microsoft Support: Clipchamp brand kit](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/topic/how-to-use-the-brand-kit-tool-in-clipchamp-e56bf921-7821-418d-9e8c-2e411a6a27ef)
- **CapCut** (search summary; official help not fetched): the brand kit stores colors, logos, fonts, videos, images, stickers and text templates. CapCut Teams adds shared Team Space storage and "Brand Templates". — [CapCut Teams help](https://www.capcut.com/help/benefits-of-capcut-teams)
- **Adobe MOGRT workflow (Frame.io, May 2023):**
  - Expose text, font, color dropdowns, logo variants (grouped layers) and frame-size options.
  - Link text styles across layers.
  - "Responsive Design—Time" protects in/out animations when a clip is resized.
  - Pin layers so reframing doesn't misalign.
  - Prefer Adobe Fonts, which auto-install.
  - Distribute through Creative Cloud Libraries so "everyone always has access to the newest version of all the templates—and only the newest ones."

  — [Frame.io Insider](https://blog.frame.io/2023/05/15/smooth-mogrt-workflows/)

### Inferences
- **Brand-level [brand/stable] grilling topics:**
  - Logo files and variants, placement, minimum size, clear space, and whether an animated logo exists.
  - Font families and weights, with the hierarchy (title / body / caption) and minimum mobile size.
  - Palette in hex, with primary/accent roles and text-contrast pairs.
  - A LUT or graded reference.
  - Motion language: easing, speed, how entrances and exits look.
  - Transition default.
  - Lower-third and caption templates.
  - Sonic logo and music genre/BPM.
  - VO/voice tone.
  - CTA phrasing, glossary / do-not-translate terms, forbidden terms.
  - A do/don't list.
  - Reference episodes.
- **Storage pattern for an agent-driven studio**, modeled on Descript, Clipchamp and Adobe:
  - One brand kit per client, with files (fonts, logos, LUT, audio) plus a machine-readable spec (colors, type scale, motion tokens, caption style, pacing targets, do-not list).
  - Projects *reference* or copy from it, as Clipchamp does.
  - Keep a single "latest version" source, as Adobe Libraries do.
  - Store a glossary for transcription accuracy (Descript). This is directly relevant to this repo's `transcrever.py` step.
- The per-project question reduces to "use the brand kit as is, or override X for this video?" Asking that is much cheaper than re-grilling every brand topic.

- Descript "layout pack" internals (which properties a layout exposes) were not examined.

## Q4. Reference-based alignment practices (moodboards, reference videos, style frames)

### Takeaway
Studios align visually in stages. First a mood board (feeling, colors, energy). Then style frames: 2–5 static designed key frames, which are the approval gate for art direction. Then storyboards (sequence, 2–3 revision cycles). Only then comes production. Creators instead send 3–5 reference videos with timestamped notes. Every source warns against presenting too many options at once.

### Cited Findings
- **VMG Studios, three stages:**
  - Mood boards outline "the mood or feeling of the animation: colors to use, character styles, atmosphere, energy".
  - Style frames translate that into actual design mockups; the studio delivers "two unique style frame designs".
  - Storyboards show "the full animation in a simplified comic-book-style layout" and go through 2–3 revision cycles.

  — [VMG Studios](https://blog.vmgstudios.com/animation-mood-boards-style-frames-storyboards)
- **Motion design practice (search summary, not individually verified):**
  - A treatment includes up to three concepts, mood boards and initial style frames.
  - Designers typically present 3–5 style frames, which "dictate the art direction".
  - Offering three directions at once causes "analysis paralysis"; label options so feedback can anchor to one.
  - Open a mood-board presentation by restating the brief in one sentence.

  — search results across [LinkedIn advice](https://www.linkedin.com/advice/0/what-some-best-practices-presenting-your-motion-design), [InspoAI](https://www.inspoai.io/blogs/share-moodboard-clients), [School of Motion](https://schoolofmotion.com/blog/mood-board-tools-motion-graphics)
- **Creator context:** "three to five finished videos with timestamped notes" as the reference package. — [The Creator's Assistant](https://www.thecreatorsassistant.com/how-to-hire-youtube-video-editor)
- **Series style guide** includes "Reference Timestamps" and a graded reference episode. — [Umbrella Creators](https://umbrellacreators.com/resources/youtube-series-style-guide-template)
- StudioBinder's brief puts mood boards and storyboard slides under "Visuals". — [StudioBinder](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/best-creative-brief-template-video-agencies-free-download/)

### Inferences
- **Grilling topics:**
  - [per-video] Reference videos or images, with *what specifically* to copy from each one (pacing? caption style? color?), timestamped where possible.
  - [per-video] Anti-references ("don't look like this").
  - [brand/stable] A reference episode that is the canonical "our look".
